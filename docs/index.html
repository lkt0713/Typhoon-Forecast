🌊 Ocean Heat Content
How to read this

Tropical cyclone heat potential: heat stored above 26 °C between the sea surface and the 26 °C isotherm (kJ/cm²), computed from the latest daily CMEMS (Copernicus Marine) 1/12° ocean analysis, or HYCOM (US Navy) as a backup  ·  A thick warm layer keeps the water under a typhoon warm even when it stirs the ocean, which favors strengthening; about 50 kJ/cm² (yellow and warmer) is a common threshold  ·  Black = water colder than 26 °C or land  ·  Dot = current JTWC position, solid line = track so far, dashed = JTWC forecast (dots every 24 h)

Ocean Heat Content along the JTWC Track

How to read this

Ocean heat content under the forecast center, sampled every 6 h along the latest JTWC forecast track (mean within ±0.5° of the center)  ·  Dashed line = 50 kJ/cm², a common threshold for intensification: above it the warm layer is thick enough that the storm’s own mixing does not cool the surface much  ·  Gray squares = land or water colder than 26 °C  ·  The ocean analysis is one daily snapshot, so this is the ocean the storm will meet if it follows the JTWC track, not a forecast of how the ocean changes

🌀 Vertical Wind Shear along the AIFS Track
How to read this

Difference between the 200 hPa and 850 hPa winds, averaged 200–800 km from the center, every 6 h along the AIFS-single (deterministic) forecast track — winds and track come from the same AIFS run  ·  Strong shear tilts the storm's upper and lower circulation apart and usually stops it strengthening: below 10 kt is low, 10–20 kt moderate, above 20 kt high  ·  Arrows = direction the shear points (from the low-level wind toward the upper-level wind); the storm's heaviest rain and convection tend to sit downshear-left  ·  Data: ECMWF Open Data

Read the intensities here as a trend, not as a forecast of peak winds. For how strong the storm will get, use the official forecasts (JTWC / JMA / CWA) in Summary; use these curves for whether it strengthens or weakens, and when. About

How to read this site

This site gathers AI and physics-based ensemble forecasts for Western Pacific tropical cyclones and redraws them in one common style every 10–15 minutes, next to the official forecasts from JTWC, JMA (Japan) and CWA (Taiwan). Each storm page has five tabs: Summary (a 3-hour Himawari-9 infrared satellite loop, the ocean heat content under the storm, the vertical wind shear along the AIFS track, the 500 hPa steering flow, the JTWC forecast, the official forecasts side by side, and Taiwan landfall and city strike probabilities), Track (model comparison, ensemble maps, animation, an interactive map and track trend), Strike (strike probabilities by area, by period and for 34-kt winds), Intensity (intensity forecast) and Verification (track, along-/cross-track and intensity errors). One model menu at the top switches every chart to the same model. The Archive keeps every storm after it is gone, with its real track and how the forecasts did.
