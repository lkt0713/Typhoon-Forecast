About

How to read this site

This site gathers AI and physics-based ensemble forecasts for Western Pacific tropical cyclones and redraws them in one common style every 10–15 minutes, next to the official forecasts from JTWC, JMA (Japan) and CWA (Taiwan). Each storm page has five tabs: Summary (a 3-hour Himawari-9 infrared satellite loop, the ocean heat content under the storm, the vertical wind shear along the AIFS track, the 500 hPa steering flow, the JTWC forecast, the official forecasts side by side, and Taiwan landfall and city strike probabilities), Track (model comparison, ensemble maps, animation, an interactive map and track trend), Strike (strike probabilities by area, by period and for 34-kt winds), Intensity (intensity forecast) and Verification (track, along-/cross-track and intensity errors). One model menu at the top switches every chart to the same model. The Archive keeps every storm after it is gone, with its real track and how the forecasts did.

Pillar's Tropical Cyclone Forecast System

Ensemble track forecasts from DeepMind WeatherNext (WN3-C, WN2-C r2, WN2-C r1, WN2-C 1000, GENC), ECMWF Open Data (AIFS-ENS + AIFS-single, IFS ENS + HRES) and NOAA NCEP (AIGEFS + AIGFS, GEFS + GFS).
Official forecasts from JTWC, JMA and CWA; satellite imagery from Himawari-9 (JMA), via NOAA Open Data on AWS; ocean heat content from Copernicus Marine (CMEMS), with HYCOM as backup. Steering flow and vertical wind shear are from AIFS-single (ECMWF Open Data). Data refreshed automatically.
