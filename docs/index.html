🌊 Ocean Heat Content
How to read this

Tropical cyclone heat potential: heat stored above 26 °C between the sea surface and the 26 °C isotherm (kJ/cm²), computed from the latest daily CMEMS (Copernicus Marine) 1/12° ocean analysis, or HYCOM (US Navy) as a backup  ·  A thick warm layer keeps the water under a typhoon warm even when it stirs the ocean, which favors strengthening; about 50 kJ/cm² (yellow and warmer) is a common threshold  ·  Black = water colder than 26 °C or land  ·  Dot = current JTWC position, solid line = track so far, dashed = JTWC forecast (dots every 24 h)

More measures

Along-track error: measured along the storm's real direction of motion, is the forecast position ahead of (+) or behind (−) the real storm? A steady + means the model moves the storm too fast, − too slow. The small |e| is the average size ignoring the sign. Only counted while the storm moves at least 5 km/h; when it stalls or loops its direction is unreliable.

About

How to read this site

This site gathers AI and physics-based ensemble forecasts for Western Pacific tropical cyclones and redraws them in one common style every 10 minutes, next to the official forecasts from JTWC, JMA (Japan) and CWA (Taiwan). Each storm page has five tabs: Summary (a 3-hour Himawari-9 infrared satellite loop, the official forecasts side by side, Taiwan landfall and city strike probabilities, and the ocean heat content under the storm), Track (model comparison, ensemble maps, animation, an interactive map, track trend, the JTWC forecast and the 500 hPa steering flow), Strike (strike probabilities by area, by period and for 34-kt winds), Intensity (intensity forecast) and Scores (track, along-/cross-track and intensity errors). One model menu at the top switches every chart to the same model. The Archive keeps every storm after it is gone, with its real track and how the forecasts did.
