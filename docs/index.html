How to read this

Himawari-9 infrared (band 13, 10.4 µm) for the last 3 hours, one frame every 10 minutes, centered on the storm; it opens on the latest frame  ·  Colors show cloud-top temperature: the colder the top, the taller the cloud and the stronger the convection; a warm dot ringed by the coldest colors is the eye and eyewall  ·  Centered on the latest JTWC position (warnings come every 6 h, so the storm may have moved a little since); the track and JTWC forecast are on the ocean heat content map below  ·  Raw data from the NOAA/JMA copy on AWS, observation time in the title of each frame

🌊 Ocean Heat Content
How to read this

Tropical cyclone heat potential: heat stored above 26 °C between the sea surface and the 26 °C isotherm (kJ/cm²), computed from the latest daily CMEMS (Copernicus Marine) 1/12° ocean analysis, or HYCOM (US Navy) as a backup  ·  A thick warm layer keeps the water under a typhoon warm even when it stirs the ocean, which favors strengthening; about 50 kJ/cm² (yellow and warmer) is a common threshold  ·  Black = water colder than 26 °C or land  ·  Dot = current JTWC position, solid line = track so far, dashed = JTWC forecast (dots every 24 h)

Ocean Heat Content along the JTWC Track

How to read this

Ocean heat content under the forecast center, sampled every 6 h along the latest JTWC forecast track (mean within ±0.5° of the center)  ·  Dashed line = 50 kJ/cm², a common threshold for intensification: above it the warm layer is thick enough that the storm’s own mixing does not cool the surface much  ·  Gray squares = land or water colder than 26 °C  ·  The ocean analysis is one daily snapshot, so this is the ocean the storm will meet if it follows the JTWC track, not a forecast of how the ocean changes

🌀 Vertical Wind Shear along the AIFS Track
How to read this

Difference between the 200 hPa and 850 hPa winds, averaged 200–800 km from the center, every 6 h along the AIFS-single (deterministic) forecast track — winds and track come from the same AIFS run  ·  Strong shear tilts the storm's upper and lower circulation apart and usually stops it strengthening: below 10 kt is low, 10–20 kt moderate, above 20 kt high  ·  Arrows = direction the shear points (from the low-level wind toward the upper-level wind); the storm's heaviest rain and convection tend to sit downshear-left  ·  Data: ECMWF Open Data

🌬️ Steering Flow (500 hPa)
How to read this

AIFS-single 500 hPa streamlines, wind speed (shading) and height (gray, every 30 gpm)  ·  Red line = 5880 gpm, the usual edge of the subtropical high: typhoons tend to move around it, west while the high extends west, turning north where it ends  ·  Navy line and dot = AIFS-single track and the storm's position at that time

🌀 Wind Radii Forecast
How to read this

In the style of the JTWC warning graphic  ·  Navy line = ensemble-mean track (dots every 12 h, time and mean wind every 24 h)  ·  Outlines = 34-, 50- and 64-kt wind radii every 12 h out to 120 h, from the median radius of all members in each quadrant (members that never reach that wind count as 0)  ·  Bold, filled = wind radii now  ·  Radii come from each model’s own tracker: WeatherNext, ECMWF (IFS / AIFS, thresholds 18 / 26 / 33 m/s) and NOAA (GEFS / AIGEFS)  ·  Model winds run low, AI models especially, so the 50- and 64-kt outlines are often smaller than in the official warning or missing

How to read this

📍 Risk at a location
How to read this

Computed in your browser from every ensemble member's track, nothing is sent anywhere  ·  Chance = share of members whose center passes within 110 km (the same 1° rule as the strike maps)  ·  Closest approach and timing are medians over all members; wind is the median member intensity at that moment  ·  This is the chance of the center coming near, not of damaging winds: a Cat 3 passing 100 km away is still dangerous, and model intensities run weak

⚠️

Read the intensities here as a trend, not as a forecast of peak winds. The AI models (WN3-C, WN2-C, GENC, AIFS, AIGFS) run at about 0.25° and cannot resolve the eyewall, so strong typhoons usually come out tens of knots too weak; the ECMWF and GFS ensembles also run weak. For how strong the storm will get, use the official forecasts (JTWC / JMA / CWA) in Summary; use these curves for whether it strengthens or weakens, and when. The Verification tab shows each model’s actual intensity bias for this storm.

About

How to read this site

This site gathers AI and physics-based ensemble forecasts for Western Pacific tropical cyclones and redraws them in one common style every 10–15 minutes, next to the official forecasts from JTWC, JMA (Japan) and CWA (Taiwan). Each storm page has five tabs: Summary (a 3-hour Himawari-9 infrared satellite loop, the ocean heat content under the storm, the vertical wind shear along the AIFS track, the 500 hPa steering flow, the JTWC forecast, the official forecasts side by side, and Taiwan landfall and city strike probabilities), Track (model comparison, ensemble maps, animation, an interactive map and track trend), Strike (strike probabilities by area, by period and for 34-kt winds), Intensity (intensity forecast) and Verification (track, along-/cross-track and intensity errors). One model menu at the top switches every chart to the same model. The Archive keeps every storm after it is gone, with its real track and how the forecasts did.

⚠️Notice

Not for operational use. For official warnings, follow your national meteorological agency.

Probabilities on this site are the share of ensemble members that forecast an outcome — a measure of how much the models agree, not an official forecast probability. A deterministic run (AIFS-single, ECMWF HRES, AIGFS, GFS) is a single possible scenario.

🔔Alerts

New systems, naming, intensity-category changes, forecast shifts of 150 km or more and Taiwan landfall chance changes of 10 points or more, as soon as an update notices them.
